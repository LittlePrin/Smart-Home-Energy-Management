This report presents a mathematical model and computational pipeline for managing energy flows in a smart home system comprising 812.4 kWh of monthly rooftop photovoltaic (PV) generation, a 5 kWh lithium-ion battery, grid connection under time-of-use tariffs, and an electric vehicle (EV). The simulation covers 30 summer days at 30-minute resolution (1440 timesteps) with 29 EV charging events. Two dispatch policies are formulated, implemented in MATLAB, and compared: a rule-based self-consumption heuristic (Policy 1) and a linear-programme (LP) cost-minimisation strategy (Policy 2). Over the 30-day horizon, Policy 2 achieves a lower net electricity cost by exploiting time-varying import tariffs (range: ~10–37 p/kWh) to shift battery and EV charging into cheap overnight windows. Eleven independent verification checks confirm that both policies satisfy all physical laws and operational constraints. Key engineering insights are drawn from four simulation output figures, including the observation that the global LP pre-charges the battery on low-demand days at a short-term cost increase, delivering net savings over the full horizon.
